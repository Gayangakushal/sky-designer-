# A Practical Digital Marketing Plan for a Sri Lankan SME

A useful SME digital marketing plan connects one business objective with a defined audience, relevant offer, suitable conversion path and a small set of measurable channel activities. It does not begin by opening accounts on every platform. Start with what the business needs to change, decide how customers make the decision, assign a role to the website, content, social media and paid media, then review evidence each month.

The framework below does not prescribe a universal budget or channel mix. It helps a Sri Lankan SME make priorities with the information and resources it actually has.

## How should a Sri Lankan SME create a digital marketing plan?

1. Choose one primary business objective.
2. Define the priority audience and decision journey.
3. Make the offer and next action clear.
4. Prepare the website or landing path.
5. Assign a specific role to social media.
6. Build content around customer decisions.
7. Decide whether paid media has a useful role.
8. Define measures, data limitations and owners.
9. Test one important assumption at a time.
10. Review evidence and actions each month.

The following sections explain each step without prescribing a universal platform mix, posting frequency or budget.

## Step 1: choose one primary business objective

“Grow online” is too broad to guide action. Choose a commercial priority that can be described clearly.

Examples include:

- increase suitable enquiries for a priority service;
- support bookings or registrations;
- introduce a new product or location;
- improve repeat purchase or customer communication;
- make the business easier to find and understand;
- replace inconsistent promotional activity with a planned system.

Define the outcome in the language the business uses. Then identify how it will be observed: a verified enquiry, appointment, order, registration or another appropriate action.

Avoid setting several unrelated priorities as equal. A focused plan can still support secondary benefits, but its main decisions should follow one objective.

## Step 2: define the audience and decision journey

Describe the people most relevant to the objective.

Record:

- the problem or need they recognise;
- what may trigger them to look for help;
- questions they ask before deciding;
- evidence they need to trust a provider;
- common reasons they delay or reject the offer;
- channels or people involved in the decision;
- language and accessibility needs;
- what happens before and after an enquiry.

Use real customer conversations, sales records, support questions and website information where available. Do not invent a persona because it looks complete in a presentation.

## Step 3: make the offer clear

Marketing cannot compensate indefinitely for an offer customers do not understand.

The plan should state:

- what is being offered;
- who it is for;
- the problem it addresses;
- what is included and excluded;
- why the business is credible;
- the next action;
- any genuine availability, eligibility or operational limits.

Avoid unsupported “best,” “leading” or guaranteed-result language. Use evidence the business can verify: process, experience, approved work, customer information or documented results with context.

## Step 4: prepare the website or landing path

Every campaign needs a place where interested people can understand the offer and take the next step. This may be a website page, landing page, form, phone call or message flow depending on the business.

Check:

- Does the page answer the main customer question?
- Is the offer clear on mobile?
- Is trust evidence visible?
- Is the next action easy to find?
- Do forms ask only useful questions?
- Are contact details accurate?
- Can the team respond to the resulting enquiries?
- Is useful activity measured?

If the current website cannot support the plan, review the [Website Design and Development service](/services/website-design-development) or improve the relevant page before spending heavily on traffic.

## Step 5: assign a role to social media

Social media should have a defined job. Depending on the business, it may:

- explain products or services;
- answer recurring questions;
- show approved work or process;
- maintain regular brand presence;
- introduce offers or events;
- support customer conversations;
- provide creative learning for wider campaigns.

Choose channels according to the audience, content capacity and business purpose. Do not assume every SME needs every channel.

Define who plans, creates, approves, publishes and responds. The [Social Media Management service](/services/social-media-management) describes one external support option; the companion guide on [in-house versus agency social media](/blog/in-house-social-media-team-vs-agency) helps assess the operating model once published.

## Step 6: build content around customer decisions

Content should help the audience understand, evaluate or act—not simply fill a calendar.

Useful themes can include:

- problem and need education;
- service or product explanation;
- process and what to expect;
- common objections;
- approved work examples;
- practical guidance;
- team or business trust information;
- offer and action content.

For each item, define the audience question, useful answer, format, channel and intended next action. One strong idea may become a website section, article, video, social post and sales resource when planned deliberately.

## Step 7: decide whether paid media has a role

Paid media can distribute an offer or message to a defined audience, but it should not be added automatically.

Before using it, confirm:

- the objective and conversion path;
- the offer and creative material;
- a suitable landing or enquiry experience;
- a media budget separate from management and production costs;
- who manages and monitors the campaign;
- how lead or sales quality will be recorded;
- whether the business can respond to demand;
- what should be learned during the campaign.

Review the [Performance Advertising service](/services/performance-advertising) and the guide to [Facebook and Instagram advertising cost factors](/blog/facebook-instagram-ads-cost-sri-lanka) when that draft is published. Neither provides a universal budget for every SME.

## Step 8: create a simple measurement plan

Measurement begins with definitions.

Create a table like this for the business:

| Plan element | Evidence to review | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website or landing path | Relevant visits, form actions, calls or bookings where measurable | `[Assign owner]` |
| Social content | Useful responses, recurring questions, enquiries or site actions | `[Assign owner]` |
| Paid media | Spend, defined outcomes, quality and downstream stages | `[Assign owner]` |
| Sales follow-up | Contact, qualification, booking, sale and loss reasons | `[Assign owner]` |
| Content production | Delivered assets, approvals, reuse and observed learning | `[Assign owner]` |

State attribution limits. Platform and analytics reports may not show every offline decision, and recorded activity does not always prove causation.

## Step 9: test one important assumption at a time

A test should answer a decision question.

Examples:

- Does the audience respond more clearly to the problem or the outcome?
- Does a page answer enough questions before the form?
- Which content format explains the offer best?
- Which enquiry question helps identify suitability?
- Where do prospects stop in the journey?

Document:

1. the hypothesis;
2. what changes;
3. what stays stable;
4. the evidence used;
5. the review condition;
6. the next action.

Do not call every content change a test. If several variables change, treat the result as directional rather than conclusive.

## Step 10: run a monthly review

A monthly review should turn activity into decisions. It does not require a complicated dashboard.

Ask:

- What was planned and completed?
- Which customer questions or objections appeared?
- Which content or offers produced useful responses?
- What happened after enquiries arrived?
- Which measurement gaps prevented a conclusion?
- What should stop, continue or change?
- Which business priority matters next month?

Record decisions and assign owners. Do not report only numbers without explaining what they mean for the plan.

## Common planning mistakes

- Starting with channels instead of a business objective.
- Copying another company's budget or content frequency.
- Promoting an unclear offer.
- Sending traffic to a weak page or unavailable sales process.
- Treating social media, paid media and the website as unrelated activities.
- Measuring lead volume without quality or follow-up.
- Changing several variables and calling the result a test.
- Reporting activity without making a decision.
- Assigning tasks without owners or approval authority.
- Publishing claims the business cannot verify.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which digital marketing channel should an SME start with?

Start with the objective and customer journey. A business may first need a clear website page, consistent social content, paid distribution, search visibility or a better follow-up process. There is no universal first channel.

### How much should an SME spend on digital marketing?

There is no universal budget. Separate media, service, creative, website, measurement and operating costs. Build the budget around the objective, available resources and evidence rather than copying a percentage.

### Does every SME need paid advertising?

No. Paid media is useful when the objective, offer, creative, conversion path, measurement and response capacity support it. Some businesses should fix those foundations first.

### How often should the plan be reviewed?

This framework recommends a monthly operating review, with additional checks when campaigns, launches or business conditions require them. The review should produce decisions, not only reports.

### What should be measured first?

Measure the action closest to the business objective that can be recorded reliably, then track useful stages leading to it. Document what remains missing or uncertain.

### Can one person manage the whole plan?

That depends on the required strategy, content, design, website, advertising, measurement and sales coordination. Assign responsibilities according to actual skills and capacity rather than a generic role title.
