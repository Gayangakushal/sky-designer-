# How Much Does Video Production Cost in Sri Lanka?

There is no universal video production price in Sri Lanka. A quote changes according to the idea, script, preparation, filming requirements, crew, equipment, locations, talent, editing, motion graphics and number of final deliverables. A simple social content session, a set of campaign creatives and a professionally produced brand video solve different problems and should not be compared as if they have the same scope.

The most reliable way to budget is to define what the video must achieve, where it will be used and exactly what must be delivered.

## What affects video production cost in Sri Lanka?

Video production cost depends on pre-production, concept and scripting, filming time, crew, equipment, locations, talent, editing, sound, motion graphics, licensing, revisions and final deliverables. A repeatable social content session and a campaign or brand production require different resources. Compare quotations using the same production conditions, usage requirements and delivery list rather than video duration alone.

## Begin with the purpose of the video

Production choices should follow the business objective. Before discussing cameras or filming days, define:

- the audience;
- the message or offer;
- the action the viewer should take;
- the channels and placements where the asset will appear;
- the useful life of the content;
- the evidence, product or people needed on screen;
- the final formats, lengths and language versions;
- who will approve the concept and final edit.

A clear purpose prevents the business from paying for production choices that do not improve the communication.

## Three different levels of video scope

These categories are planning descriptions, not fixed packages or price levels.

| Scope | Typical purpose | Common production characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple social content | Regular product, service, educational or behind-the-scenes communication | Focused setup, repeatable formats, limited locations and efficient editing |
| Campaign creative | Support a defined launch, offer or advertising objective | Stronger concepts, message variations, planned hooks, multiple formats and testing needs |
| Professionally produced brand video | Present a broader story, identity, event or high-value message | More detailed pre-production, direction, production control, sound, lighting and post-production |

A simple asset can still require professional judgement. A larger production is not automatically more effective. The scope should match the communication problem and intended use.

## Cost factor 1: concept and creative development

The concept determines what the audience will see and why it should matter. Creative development may involve:

- clarifying the message and audience;
- reviewing the offer or campaign brief;
- developing one or more concepts;
- selecting a format and visual approach;
- writing hooks, scenes or a treatment;
- planning how one idea becomes several deliverables;
- identifying legal, operational or brand restrictions.

A business supplying an approved, practical concept may need less development than one asking the production team to define the entire direction. Confirm how many concepts or review stages the quote includes.

## Cost factor 2: scripting and pre-production

Pre-production turns the idea into a shoot that can be executed. It may include:

- script or interview-question development;
- storyboard, shot list or scene planning;
- schedule and call-sheet preparation;
- location selection and permissions;
- talent or presenter coordination;
- product, wardrobe, prop or set preparation;
- crew and equipment planning;
- travel and operational coordination;
- release, approval or compliance requirements.

Skipping preparation can create delays and expensive changes during filming or editing. The appropriate level depends on the complexity of the video.

## Cost factor 3: filming days and production time

Filming scope is affected by more than the finished video's duration. A short video can require several setups, locations or performances. A longer interview can sometimes be captured with a simpler setup.

Quote variables include:

- number of filming sessions or days;
- setup and travel requirements;
- number of scenes or products;
- lighting and sound complexity;
- location access windows;
- talent availability;
- weather or continuity considerations;
- number of variations captured;
- reshoot conditions.

Ask what counts as a filming day or session, what preparation is expected from the client and what happens if the agreed plan changes.

## Cost factor 4: crew and specialist roles

The team should reflect the work required. A focused social shoot may combine responsibilities, while a complex production may require separate direction, camera, lighting, sound, styling, production and support roles.

Do not judge a quote only by crew size. Ask:

- Which responsibilities are required?
- Who is accountable for creative direction and production decisions?
- Which roles are included?
- What must the client supply?
- Are specialist roles or assistants conditional on the final concept?

The answer should follow the approved scope rather than a standard staffing claim.

## Cost factor 5: equipment and technical requirements

Equipment should be selected for the intended result and production conditions. Requirements can include camera, lenses, stabilisation, lighting, sound, monitoring, power, storage or specialised capture.

More equipment does not automatically produce a better message. Cost changes when the concept requires:

- several camera setups;
- controlled lighting across a large space;
- clean dialogue or location sound;
- movement or stabilised shots;
- specialised product detail;
- challenging locations or conditions;
- redundant recording or data handling;
- licensed stock, music or other assets.

Ask why each significant technical requirement is needed and whether licences are included.

## Cost factor 6: locations, talent and production assets

Locations and people can introduce fees, permissions, coordination and risk.

Consider:

- owned versus hired locations;
- travel, access and parking;
- location preparation and restoration;
- actors, presenters, models or voice talent;
- usage permissions and agreed duration or territory;
- wardrobe, makeup, props, products or set dressing;
- releases for identifiable people;
- weather or backup planning.

Do not assume that talent, locations, products or permissions are included. Record responsibilities in the quotation.

## Cost factor 7: editing and post-production

Post-production shapes the captured material into final communication. Scope can include:

- selecting and organising footage;
- editing and pacing;
- colour correction or grading;
- audio cleanup and mixing;
- music or stock licensing;
- captions and subtitles;
- graphics and titles;
- visual cleanup;
- review versions and revisions;
- exports for different channels.

Editing cost is influenced by the amount and complexity of source material, not only final duration. Confirm the review stages, feedback method and final export list.

## Cost factor 8: motion graphics and animation

Motion graphics may range from simple titles and logo animation to product visualisation or fully designed sequences. The quote should distinguish basic text/graphic treatment from custom animation.

Clarify:

- which elements are animated;
- whether the design assets already exist;
- the complexity and duration of animation;
- data or product accuracy requirements;
- revision and approval stages;
- source-file or future-edit arrangements.

The [OMS T30 promotional video](/work/video-production-brand-promotion) is a published example of brand-promotion video output. It should not be used to infer the scope or cost of an unrelated production.

## Cost factor 9: number, duration and format of deliverables

One production can produce one final film or a planned library of assets. Deliverables may vary by:

- duration;
- aspect ratio;
- hook or opening;
- message or offer;
- language;
- caption treatment;
- platform or placement;
- thumbnail or still-image requirement;
- clean and branded versions.

Ask for an exact deliverables schedule. “Social media versions” is too vague if the business expects several formats and edits.

## Cost factor 10: reviews, changes and reshoots

Agree on:

- who approves the concept and script;
- who attends or approves filming;
- how feedback is consolidated;
- which edit reviews are included;
- what counts as a revision;
- what counts as a new deliverable or concept;
- when a reshoot is chargeable;
- how factual or legal corrections are handled.

Changes are less disruptive when the right people approve the concept, script and production plan before filming.

## How to compare video quotations

| Area | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Objective | Intended audience, message, action and channels |
| Concept | Creative development and directions included |
| Pre-production | Script, shot list, locations, talent and coordination |
| Production | Sessions, crew roles, equipment and client responsibilities |
| Post-production | Editing, sound, colour, captions, graphics and licences |
| Deliverables | Exact lengths, formats, versions and exports |
| Reviews | Approval points, revisions and scope-change rules |
| Rights | Talent, music, stock, source files and usage arrangements |
| Logistics | Travel, locations, products, permissions and contingencies |
| Handover | Final delivery method, storage and future-edit support |

Compare the same scope. A lower quote may exclude scripting, talent, captions, format variations or usage rights that another includes.

## How to prepare a quote-ready video brief

Include:

1. business objective and intended action;
2. audience and message;
3. channels and placements;
4. required final deliverables;
5. available products, people, locations and brand assets;
6. language, caption and accessibility needs;
7. reference examples with reasons;
8. factual, legal or brand restrictions;
9. decision-makers and approval process;
10. any fixed event, launch or availability constraints.

The production team can then recommend an approach instead of guessing what “one video” means.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why can a short video cost more than a longer one?

Final duration does not show the concept, scenes, filming setups, talent, animation or editing complexity required. Compare the complete production process and deliverables.

### Is editing included in video production?

It depends on the quotation. Confirm editing, sound, colour, captions, graphics, music or stock licences, review rounds and export formats.

### Can one filming session create several social media assets?

It can when derivatives are planned before filming and the scope includes the required shots and edits. Do not assume a fixed asset quantity; list each expected deliverable.

### Is professional equipment the main cost driver?

No single factor determines cost. Concept, planning, crew, locations, talent, filming, post-production, animation, licences and deliverables can all affect the quote.

### Who owns the final video and source footage?

Ownership, permitted use, source footage, project files and third-party licences should be defined in the agreement. Do not assume they are identical across providers.

### Does a professional video guarantee better campaign results?

No. Production quality supports communication, but results also depend on the offer, audience, distribution, customer journey and measurement. Avoid guarantees based on production alone.
