# How Much Does Branding Cost in Sri Lanka?

There is no single branding price that applies to every Sri Lankan business. Cost changes with the problem being solved and the work required: a logo-only assignment is different from a complete identity involving research, visual direction, applications, guidelines, revisions and final file preparation. To compare quotations fairly, ask each provider to define the scope, process, deliverables and ownership—not only the final total.

This guide explains the factors behind a branding quotation without inventing price ranges or assuming that every business needs a complete rebrand.

## First decide whether you need a logo or a brand identity

A logo is one identifying element. A brand identity is a broader visual and verbal system that helps a business communicate consistently across relevant customer touchpoints.

A logo-focused assignment may involve a primary mark, selected variations and final files. A wider identity can also address typography, colour, imagery, layout, tone, applications and practical usage guidance.

Neither scope is automatically better. The right choice depends on the business problem.

### A logo-only scope may be appropriate when

- the business has a clear position and communication system;
- existing colours, typography and applications remain useful;
- the main issue is a missing or unusable identifying mark;
- the team can apply the result consistently;
- a larger strategic change is not required.

### A broader identity may be appropriate when

- customers struggle to understand or recognise the business;
- visuals change substantially between channels;
- the offer or audience has changed;
- the current identity does not work across necessary formats;
- internal and external teams lack practical usage rules;
- a new business needs more than a symbol to launch consistently.

A responsible provider should help clarify the scope rather than automatically recommend the largest project.

## Branding cost factor 1: the problem and scope

The first cost driver is what the project must achieve. “Create a logo” is narrower than “clarify our position and build an identity for a multi-channel launch.”

A useful brief should explain:

- why the business is considering branding work;
- what has changed or is not working;
- who the priority audience is;
- which products, services or sub-brands are involved;
- where the identity must appear;
- what existing assets may remain;
- who will review and approve the work;
- which deliverables are necessary at launch.

Scope becomes more complex when several audiences, offers, business units or applications must work within one system.

## Branding cost factor 2: research and discovery

Research helps the creative direction respond to the business rather than personal taste alone. The depth required varies.

Discovery may include:

- interviews with founders or internal stakeholders;
- review of the offer, audience and customer questions;
- audit of current identity and communication;
- review of relevant competitors and category patterns;
- clarification of positioning, personality and practical constraints;
- inventory of applications and language needs.

A small, clearly defined business may need a focused discovery process. A larger organisation with several stakeholders or a substantial change may require broader investigation. Ask what research is included, who participates and what output it produces.

Do not assume that a longer research phase is always better. It should answer decisions that matter to the identity.

## Branding cost factor 3: creative direction and identity elements

The creative scope may include some or all of the following:

- logo or wordmark development;
- primary and secondary marks;
- colour system;
- typography direction;
- image or illustration style;
- layout and graphic devices;
- icon or pattern systems;
- tone or messaging guidance;
- language or script considerations;
- accessibility and practical reproduction checks.

The quotation should state which elements are being created and how many directions or decision stages are included. Avoid comparing a provider offering one defined deliverable with another proposing a complete system as if the scopes were identical.

## Branding cost factor 4: number of applications

An identity becomes useful when it can be applied to the places customers and staff encounter it. Each application adds design, review and file-preparation work.

Applications might include:

- social media profiles and templates;
- advertising or campaign layouts;
- presentation or document templates;
- stationery;
- packaging or labels;
- signage;
- uniforms;
- website direction;
- sales materials;
- launch assets.

This is not a checklist every business must buy. Choose applications based on the actual launch and operating needs. Ask whether the quotation includes final production-ready files, visual examples or reusable templates.

## Branding cost factor 5: brand guidelines

Guidelines explain how to use the identity consistently. Their depth can range from a compact usage sheet to a more complete document covering multiple elements and applications.

Useful guidance may address:

- approved logo versions and clear space;
- colour values and combinations;
- type hierarchy;
- image or illustration direction;
- layout principles;
- examples of correct and incorrect use;
- social or campaign templates;
- language-specific applications;
- file and asset organisation.

The appropriate level depends on who will use the identity. A small owner-managed business may need concise, practical rules. A business working with several staff, suppliers or locations may need stronger documentation.

Ask who will receive the files, whether the team will be shown how to use them and how future applications should be handled.

## Branding cost factor 6: feedback, revisions and stakeholders

Revisions are affected by the clarity of the brief and the number of decision-makers. A project can become inefficient when feedback is late, contradictory or based on new requirements introduced after approval.

Before work begins, agree on:

- who supplies the brief;
- who attends discovery;
- who consolidates internal feedback;
- who has final approval;
- which review stages are included;
- what counts as a revision versus a change in scope;
- how additional work is approved;
- what happens after a stage is signed off.

More revision rounds do not necessarily create a better identity. Clear decisions and useful feedback matter more.

## Branding cost factor 7: final deliverables and ownership

A branding quotation should list exactly what the client receives.

Confirm:

- final logo formats and variations;
- colour and typography information;
- guideline documents;
- application or template files;
- editable/source-file arrangements;
- font, photography or third-party licence responsibilities;
- ownership and permitted use;
- storage and handover process;
- support for future applications, if any.

Do not assume that every provider includes source files, licensed assets or unlimited future adaptations. Clarify these items before comparing totals.

## Other factors that can change the scope

### Existing brand condition

A business with organised source files and clear decisions may need less reconstruction than one with missing assets and conflicting versions.

### Multiple languages

English, Sinhala and Tamil applications can require additional review, typography and layout decisions. Qualified language review should be provided by someone who can verify meaning, tone and accuracy—not inferred from appearance alone.

### Naming or messaging

Business naming, product naming, taglines and wider messaging are separate strategic tasks unless the proposal includes them.

### Production and rollout

Printing, signage installation, packaging production, photography, website implementation and launch campaigns may be separate from identity design. Ask where design responsibility ends and production begins.

### Urgency and dependencies

Launch dates, stakeholder availability and external production can affect planning. Do not assume a standard completion time; ask for a schedule based on the approved scope.

## How to compare branding quotations fairly

| Area | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Business problem | What the branding work is intended to solve |
| Discovery | Interviews, audits, research and outputs included |
| Creative scope | Logo-only, identity elements, directions and decision stages |
| Applications | Exact assets, templates or examples included |
| Guidelines | Depth, format and intended users |
| Revisions | Review stages, feedback process and scope-change rules |
| Deliverables | Formats, editable files, licences and handover |
| Ownership | Rights, third-party assets and future use |
| Production | What is designed versus printed, built or installed |
| Support | Training, rollout or future adaptations if included |

If two totals differ significantly, compare the rows before deciding that one provider is expensive. The scope may be different.

## How to prepare for a branding quotation

Provide:

1. a short business and offer description;
2. the reason for the project;
3. priority audiences;
4. existing assets and known problems;
5. required launch applications;
6. relevant languages;
7. decision-makers and approval owner;
8. external production needs;
9. any fixed commercial or regulatory constraints;
10. examples of what the team finds useful, with reasons rather than instructions to copy.

A better brief allows providers to quote the same problem and reduces unexpected additions later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is logo design the same as branding?

No. A logo is an identity element. Branding work may also include research, positioning context, colour, typography, imagery, layout, applications and guidelines. Some businesses need only a focused logo assignment; others need the wider system.

### Why do branding quotations vary so much?

They may cover different discovery, strategy, identity elements, applications, guidelines, revisions, file rights and rollout work. Compare deliverables and process before comparing totals.

### Does every business need full brand guidelines?

No. The appropriate depth depends on how many people and suppliers use the identity, how many applications exist and how much consistency guidance they need.

### Should editable files be included?

That depends on the agreement. Confirm final formats, editable/source files, third-party licences, ownership and future-use arrangements in writing.

### How many revisions should a branding project include?

There is no universal number. The proposal should define review stages and scope-change rules. Clear briefs and consolidated feedback are more useful than unlimited unfocused revisions.

### Can the existing identity be refreshed instead of replaced?

Possibly. An audit may show that selected elements can remain while usability or consistency problems are corrected. A provider should understand what equity is worth preserving before recommending a full change.
